# Candidates — One-Star Review of Something Essential

Judge brief (in order): (1) a one-star review of an everyday essential,
deadpan and unmistakably about the thing itself; (2) the sharpest true
grievance, the small daily annoyance everyone recognises but has never seen
written down; (3) holds the deadpan — no winking, no explaining the joke, no
exclamation marks; (4) turns in the last few words, the sting arriving after
the complaint has been read straight; (5) tight and specific, one joke told
cleanly, no filler, no second joke crowding the first. Max 160 characters.

## A — Gravity (leading candidate)

> Gravity is free, always on, and has never once caught anything I dropped. One star.

- Length: 89 characters.
- (1) Names the thing in the first word; the "One star." sign-off makes the
  review form unmistakable. Deadpan throughout.
- (2) Grievance: gravity is framed as a service whose one job should be
  catching, and it has never done it. Everyone has watched something fall
  and break; nobody has written the complaint this way.
- (3) Deadpan holds: flat consumer-review language ("free, always on"),
  no exclamation, no wink, nothing explained.
- (4) Turn lands in the last clause: "has never once caught anything
  I dropped" reframes the whole complaint after it has been read straight.
- (5) One joke, one sentence plus sign-off, every word earns its place.

## B — Sleep

> Sleep takes a third of my life, offers no refund, and still wakes me up tired. One star.

- Length: 96 characters.
- (1) Clearly about sleep, clearly a one-star review. Deadpan.
- (2) Grievance is real (waking tired despite a full night) but familiar:
  "sleep doesn't work" is a complaint everyone has already heard.
- (3) Deadpan holds.
- (4) Turn: "still wakes me up tired" arrives last, but it is the expected
  ending rather than a surprise reframe.
- (5) Tight, one joke. Weaker than A on (2) and (4).

## C — Oxygen

> Oxygen is free, invisible, and everywhere, yet I still have to do all the breathing myself. One star.

- Length: 104 characters.
- (1) Clearly about oxygen, deadpan, one-star form.
- (2) Grievance is weak: breathing is automatic, so "I have to do it
  myself" is not a true daily annoyance anyone recognises — it is a
  stretch, and the judge's criterion 2 asks for a true grievance.
- (3) Deadpan holds.
- (4) Turn: "do all the breathing myself" is funny, but it leans on the
  reader noticing breathing is involuntary, which blunts the sting.
- (5) Tight, one joke. Weaker than A on (2).

## Verdict

A wins. It has the sharpest true grievance (criterion 2, the most important
after the form itself) and the cleanest turn (criterion 4): gravity as a
negligent service that never catches anything. B is the safe second choice;
C's grievance is not quite true. Keep A unless a rewrite beats it on the
criteria — none of the alternates does.

## Refinement of A

First draft: "Gravity is free, always on, and has never once caught anything I dropped. One star."
- "free, always on" sets up the service framing in four words.
- "has never once caught anything I dropped" is the sting; "never once"
  is doing the work and stays. "One star." is the deadpan close, the
  review-form equivalent of closing a ticket.
- No word cut: "free" (the price complaint), "always on" (the uptime
  boast), "never once" (the failure record), "anything I dropped" (the
  specific evidence). Final text unchanged from the draft.
